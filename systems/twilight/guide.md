# Playing Twilight: 2000 (4th edition)

## The game
1st of July 2000. World War III went nuclear, and the last order from command was "Good luck, you're on your own." The characters are soldiers (and some civilians) stranded in war-torn Poland or Sweden, trying to survive, help others and find a way home. It is a survival sandbox: food, fuel, ammunition and radiation matter.

## Rolling
Attributes (STR, AGL, INT, EMP) and skills are rated **A to D**: A is a d12, B a d10, C a d8, D a d6. A skill at F means no skill die.

- Roll the **attribute die and the skill die** together. Each die showing **6 or more** is a success; on a d10 or d12 a **10 or more** is **two** successes.
- Modifiers step dice up or down (D to C to B to A and back) rather than adding to the roll. The skill die moves first: a +1 gives an untrained skill a D, and only once the skill die is A does the attribute die rise. A -1 lowers the skill die first, and the attribute die once there is no skill die (never below D). The sheet's Modifier does this.
- **Push**: once, reroll every die that didn't succeed. After a push, each 1 on a die costs a point of damage (STR, AGL) or stress (INT, EMP).
- Some rolls use one die alone, such as coolness under fire (CUF).

## Combat
- **Initiative**: deal one card from 1 to 10 to every combatant; act in order.
- **Attacks**: AGL + Ranged Combat for small arms, STR + Heavy Weapons for crew-served weapons, STR + Close Combat in melee.
- **Ammo dice**: firing more than a single shot adds yellow ammo dice. Each 6 on them is an extra hit, but only when the attribute and skill dice hit; ammo sixes never make a hit on their own. Every die is a round spent, and a push keeps the ammo dice as rolled, and 1s can mean a weapon problem.
- **Hit location** (d6): 1 legs, 2-4 torso, 5 arms, 6 head. Armor at the location stops damage up to its rating and is worn down by hits.
- Extra successes add damage or effects. Damage counts against **hit capacity** ((STR die + AGL die) / 4); at it the character is **broken** and rolls a critical injury.
- Being shot at forces a **suppression** roll with coolness under fire, or the character gains stress and may be pinned.

## Stress and radiation
Stress counts against **stress capacity** ((INT die + EMP die) / 4). **Rads** build up from fallout; some become permanent and bring radiation sickness.

## Recovery
Rest heals damage and stress slowly; critical injuries need Medical Aid and days of healing.

## Vehicles
Vehicles are their own entity type with armor by facing, fuel and hit capacity.
