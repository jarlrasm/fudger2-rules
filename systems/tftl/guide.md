# Playing Tales from the Loop

## The game
An alternate 1980s, where giant robots roam the fields and strange machines hum around a secret research facility, the Loop. The players are **kids** aged 10 to 15. They solve mysteries the grown-ups can't see, while dealing with home, school and friends. Kids are never killed: the danger is to their bodies, feelings and friendships.

## Rolling
- Roll a d6 per point of **attribute + skill**. Each **six** is a success; one is usually enough, more give extra effect.
- The roll asks for **extra dice**: an item helps with +1 to +3 (an iconic item +2), a friend helping gives +1. A negative number takes dice away.
- Each marked **condition** costs one die; the sheet already takes them off.
- **Pushing**: reroll every die that was not a six, then mark a condition. Or spend a **Luck point** to reroll without one.
- **Pride**: once a mystery, turn a failed roll into a success with one six.

## Attributes and skills
Body (Sneak, Force, Move), Tech (Tinker, Program, Calculate), Heart (Contact, Charm, Lead), Mind (Investigate, Comprehend, Empathize). The four attributes add up to the kid's age. Luck points are 15 minus age.

## Trouble
There is no initiative or combat. When kids face danger, the GM sets how many successes they need (often the NPC's or creature's *Difficulty*). A failure marks a condition: Upset, Scared, Exhausted, Injured. A kid with all four who fails again is **broken** and can do nothing until helped. Time with the **anchor** or a night at home heals conditions (the *Go home to the anchor* button). The *New mystery* button brings back Pride and Luck.

## Everyday life
Every mystery is played between school, chores and family. Each kid's **problem** and **drive** shape scenes, and relationships between kids matter as much as the mystery.
