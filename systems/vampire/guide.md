# Playing Vampire: the Masquerade (5th edition)

## Rolling
Build a pool of **d10s**, usually an Attribute plus a Skill (each skill row has a picker for the attribute that fits the task). Each die showing **6 or more** is a success. Each roll asks for the **Difficulty**, the number of successes needed (leave it blank when only the GM knows it); set Modifier on the sheet for bonus or penalty dice.

- **Critical:** every pair of 10s counts as four successes instead of two.
- **Hunger dice:** as many dice as the vampire's Hunger are red. A critical with a 10 on a hunger die is a **messy critical**: they succeed, but the Beast shows. A failed roll with a 1 on a hunger die is a **bestial failure**: the Beast takes over.
- **Willpower:** after a roll, spend 1 superficial Willpower to reroll up to three of the non-hunger dice. The roll window offers *Reroll with Willpower*, which also marks the superficial Willpower on the sheet.
- **Rouse checks:** using Blood (Disciplines, waking, healing) rolls a d10; on 1–5 Hunger rises by one.

## Conflict
Combat is opposed pools in an order the Storyteller sets, usually by who is ready.

1. Attacks oppose the target's defence (Dexterity + Athletics to dodge, or a fighting pool). Damage is the margin plus the weapon's modifier.
2. Mortal weapons give vampires **superficial** damage (halved, rounded up); fire, sunlight and fangs deal **aggravated** damage.
3. When the Health track is full of damage, the character is **Impaired** (−2 to physical pools). A vampire with all boxes aggravated meets Final Death.
4. Spend Rouse checks to heal superficial damage between turns.

## Humanity and Hunger
Feeding lowers Hunger; the Beast grows as it rises. Breaking the Chronicle Tenets and Convictions earns **Stains**; at the end of a session, roll the Remorse pool (one die per empty Humanity box) to keep Humanity.

## Style
Personal horror. Each night pits the vampire's desires and Hunger against the people they love and the Masquerade that keeps them hidden.
