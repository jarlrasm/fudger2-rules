# Playing Dune: Adventures in the Imperium

## The game
The Imperium of Frank Herbert's Dune: the players are members of a noble **House** and its retainers, struggling for power, honour and the spice through intrigue, espionage, duels and war. Each character serves the House in a **role** (Heir, Spymaster, Swordmaster, Mentat...).

## Tests
A test uses one **skill** (what you do: Battle, Communicate, Discipline, Move, Understand) and one **drive** (why you do it: Duty, Faith, Justice, Power, Truth), each rated 4 to 8.

- Roll **2d20** against a **target number = skill + drive**. Each die at or under the target is a **success**.
- A **1** is always two successes. With a relevant **focus**, every die at or under the skill rating is two successes.
- The GM sets a **difficulty** from 0 to 5: the successes needed. Extra successes become **Momentum**, shared by the group, which buys extra dice, information and effects.
- Each **20** rolled is a **complication**.
- Buy up to three extra d20s with Momentum, by giving the GM **Threat**, or with **Determination**.

## Drives and statements
A drive of 6 or more has a **statement**, a belief such as "Duty: the House comes first". If the statement doesn't fit the action, the player may **challenge** it: roll anyway at the cost of crossing it out later.

## Conflict
Conflicts (duels, skirmishes, espionage, intrigue, warfare) are fought with **assets**: weapons, contacts, plans and secrets placed in zones. There are no hit points: a successful attack **defeats** a minor opponent; notable and major characters may spend Threat (or players Momentum or Determination) to avoid it, or take a complication such as a wound.

## Turns
The players act first; then the initiative passes between sides. Pay Momentum (or Threat for the GM) to keep it and act again.

## Determination
Each adventure starts with one point. Spend it for an automatic critical die, a reroll, or to act boldly in the service of a drive.

## Houses
The House is its own entity type: its type, homeworld, banner, domains, allies and enemies, and the Threat the GM begins with.
