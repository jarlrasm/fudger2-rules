# Playing Fudge

## The ladder
Traits are words on a ladder: Terrible, Poor, Mediocre, Fair, Good, Great, Superb. Fair is an ordinary trained adult. Attributes (Str, Dex, Con, Int) and skills all use the ladder.

## Rolling
Roll 4dF (four Fudge dice, each -1, 0 or +1) and move that many steps along the ladder from the trait. A Good trait with a roll of -1 gives Fair. The sheet does this when a trait is clicked, and the chat shows the result.

- **Unopposed:** set a difficulty on the ladder (Fair for ordinary tasks, Good or Great for hard ones). Meeting it succeeds. Each step above or below is a better or worse result.
- **Opposed:** both sides roll, and the higher result wins. The difference is the relative degree.

## Combat
1. Each side rolls its fighting skill, opposed. The winner hits if they beat the loser by at least one step.
2. Damage is the relative degree, plus the attacker's offensive factors (weapon, strength), minus the defender's defensive factors (armour, toughness).
3. Compare the damage to the Injury Levels on the sheet: 1-3 Scratch, 4-6 Hurt, 7-9 Very hurt, 10-12 Incapacitated, 13+ Near death.
4. Mark the box in Damage. A full box spills into the next level. Hurt gives -1 to actions, Very hurt -2.

The Heal button moves every wound one level down.

## Movement
Fudge has no fixed distance. A combat round is a few seconds. As a rule of thumb, a character can walk about 5 metres and still act, or run about 15 metres and do nothing else. Raise or lower that a step for fast or slow characters (a Great Dex or a Running skill, or a heavy load). In a chase, roll opposed Dex or Running.

## Initiative
The campaign settings list which traits roll initiative (by default the first one the character has). Call initiative and everyone rolls.

## Other
- **Fudge points** let a player change a result, avoid a wound or bring in a lucky break.
- **Gifts and Faults** are short descriptions. Let them matter in play: a gift may give a +1 or let something happen at all, and a fault should bring trouble.
