# Fudge Combat options

Optional rules from the Fudge SRD's combat chapter: hit location (4.35), damage rolls (4.61 and 4.63), and stun and knockout (4.62).

## Damage factor
The damage factor is the attacker's offensive factors minus the defender's defensive factors:
- **Offensive:** Strength above Fair (+1 for Good), the weapon's Damage, and Strength Scale.
- **Defensive:** Damage Capacity above Fair, the armour's Damage Capacity, and Mass Scale.

Work it out once per fight.

## Damage rolls
Pick one roll for the campaign. Each asks for the **relative degree** (how much the attack won by) and the **damage factor**.
- **Situational** takes the relative degree plus the damage factor and adds 4dF. The die never more than doubles positive damage and never drops it below 1. When the damage is 0 or less, the roll can raise it to 1 at most. The result is read on the sheet's track: 1-2 Scratch, 3-4 Hurt, 5-6 Very hurt, 7-8 Incapacitated, 9+ Near death.
- **Min-Mid-Max** rolls 3d6 and doesn't add the relative degree. A higher damage factor (3+, 6+) and relative degree (4+, 6+) read a higher die: lowest, middle, highest, highest + lowest, or all three. A negative factor reads one lower. The result is read on the same track.

A relative degree of +1 is a **graze**: no damage with a negative factor, a Scratch up to factor 4, and Hurt from 5.

## Stun and knockout
Declare before the roll that the blow is to stun, such as with the flat of a blade, and use **Stunning**:
- **Hurt** is a Stun: no attack or all-out defence, and -1 to defend for one turn. It stays marked, so a second stun is Very stunned.
- **Very hurt** is Very stunned: no attacks and -2 to everything for two rounds.
- **Incapacitated or worse** knocks the target out. They recover with time, not healing.

A Good blow to the head that wins the round may knock out at once, if the GM allows. Press Recover after the fight.

A fighter may also **pull a punch**: name the worst wound the blow can do, such as a Scratch for first blood.

## Hit location
Better relative degrees land better blows. To aim at a body part, say so before rolling. The GM names the relative degree needed, usually 2 to 4, and more for an eye. Win by less and the defender picks where the blow lands.

Note the wound under **Location wounds**:
- **Scratched:** no effect.
- **Hurt:** -1 to use that part. A hurt sword arm is -1 to fight.
- **Incapacitated:** the part can't be used. After the battle, decide whether it heals.
