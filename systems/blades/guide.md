# Playing Blades in the Dark

## Action rolls
A player says what their scoundrel does; the GM picks the action, the **position** (Controlled, Risky, Desperate) and the **effect** (Limited, Standard, Great). Set those on the sheet, then roll the action: **one d6 per dot**, and read the **highest die**.

- **6:** full success. **Two 6s:** critical, with increased effect. **4–5:** partial success, with a consequence. **1–3:** bad outcome.
- With zero dots, roll 2d6 and take the **lowest**.
- Add **Bonus Dice** for assistance (another player takes 1 stress), for pushing yourself (take 2 stress), or for a Devil's Bargain.
- Consequences depend on position: reduced effect, a complication, lost opportunity, a worse position, or **harm**.

## Resistance and stress
A scoundrel can **resist** a consequence: roll the Insight, Prowess or Resolve resistance (one die per action with a dot in that group). The consequence is reduced or avoided, and the scoundrel takes **6 minus the highest die** in stress (a critical clears 1 stress). At 9 stress they take a **trauma** and leave the scene; the fourth trauma retires them.

## Harm
Level 1 harm (less effect), level 2 (−1d, taken off action rolls by the sheet while either level 2 box is filled), level 3 (needs help to act), level 4 is fatal. Armor can absorb a consequence. Healing fills the Healing clock during downtime.

## Clocks and scores
Track obstacles, threats and long projects as **clocks** of 4, 6 or 8 segments. A score begins with a plan, a detail, and an **engagement roll** (a fortune roll on the crew sheet), then cuts to the action. Use flashbacks instead of planning ahead.

## Downtime
After a score: payoff (coin and rep), heat, entanglements, then downtime activities such as indulging a vice to clear stress, recovering, training, and long-term projects.

## Style
Players are bold scoundrels; the GM is a fan of the characters and of the city of Duskwall. Play to find out what happens, and let failure lead to trouble rather than dead ends.
