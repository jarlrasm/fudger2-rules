# Torchbearer 2nd edition

Adventurers go into dark places for loot because they have nothing else. Play it hard: food, light and space in the pack run short, and every test costs time.

## Tests
A test rolls an ability or skill in d6s. Each 4, 5 or 6 is a success. The GM sets an **Obstacle** (Ob); meet it to pass. On a failure the GM picks: the adventurer gets what they wanted but takes a **condition**, or a **twist** makes things worse. Versus tests compare successes. Help from others (+1D each), gear, wises and traits add dice. Each **factor** in the situation adds 1 to the Ob; being Exhausted and being in dim light or darkness are factors, and the sheet adds them. Fresh gives +1D, Injured and Sick take 1D off; the sheet counts those too, but not for Resources and Circles.

Without the skill, **Beginner's Luck** rolls Will or Health (the skill's BL column), and the dice are halved, rounded up. Afraid adventurers can't use Beginner's Luck or help. Using a skill at 0 as many times as your Nature lets you learn it. Ratings advance with passes equal to the rating and fails one less; the Advance column says when.

**Nature** is the stock's instinctive side, with three descriptors. Persona taps Nature to add it to a roll; outside the descriptors, failing taxes Nature.

## Fate, Persona and checks
After a roll, **Fate** buys Luck: every 6 rolls again as a new die, open-ended. **Persona** before a roll adds +1D per point (up to three) or taps Nature; after a failed roll with a wise, Persona buys Of Course!, rerolling the failed dice. The roll window offers Luck when there are 6s and Of Course! when dice failed, and takes the point off. Fate with a wise buys Deeper Understanding, rerolling one failed die. Both are earned at the end of a session for playing Belief, Creed, Goal and Instinct; spent points count toward the next level. **Checks** come from using traits against yourself (as a -1D, or giving the other side +2D, or breaking a tie in their favour); they buy tests in camp and town.

## The grind and light
Every test or conflict is a **turn**. After every fourth turn's test, each adventurer takes the next condition they don't already have: Hungry and Thirsty, Exhausted, Angry, Sick, Injured, Afraid, then Dead. The sheet's Grind button does that. A torch lights two people for two turns, a lantern three for three turns on a flask of oil, a candle one for four. People beside the light but not in it are in dim light; beyond it, darkness. Use an Expedition sheet to count turns and light: Next turn moves the count and burns each light down.

## Conditions
Hungry and Thirsty: -1 disposition, cleared by eating and drinking. Angry: no wises or beneficial traits. Afraid: no help, no Beginner's Luck. Exhausted: a factor in every test but Circles, Resources and recovery. Injured and Sick: -1D to skills, Nature, Will and Health; Sick adventurers can't learn or practise. Recovery goes in this order, one at a time: Angry Will Ob 2, Afraid Will Ob 3, Exhausted Health Ob 3, Injured Health Ob 4, Sick Will Ob 3. A healer or priest can treat a failed Injured or Sick recovery. Fresh is lost with the first condition; the Fresh again button clears all of them when the GM rules a full recovery.

## Camp and town
Camp stops the grind. Roll camp events, then spend checks to recover, memorize spells, make tests and plan. Leaving camp sets the turn count back to 1. In town each adventurer pays their Lifestyle with a Resources test, shops at the market and recovers. Failing a Resources test taxes it.

## Conflicts
Fights, chases, arguments, tricks and bargains are conflicts: kill, capture, drive off, flee or pursue, convince, convince a crowd, trick, negotiate, abjure. The team's captain rolls the conflict's skill; successes plus the ability (Health or Will) are the team's **disposition**, shared out among its members. Each side scripts three actions a round, revealed one at a time: Attack against Defend is a versus test, Defend restores disposition, Feint beats Attack, Maneuver gains position or impedes. Weapons add dice or successes to some actions; armor takes off a success. A side at 0 disposition loses; the winner pays a compromise for the disposition it lost. The side with more **Might** has the upper hand in kill and capture conflicts. Monsters roll their Nature for everything.

## Inventory
Every item takes slots: head 1, neck 1, hands worn 2, hands carried 2, torso 3, belt 3, feet 1, and the pack (6 in a backpack, 3 in a satchel). The Load row adds up each place; an overloaded place shows on the map token.

## Initiative
There is none: in a conflict both sides script their actions, and outside conflicts the GM frames scenes and tests in turn.
