# Playing Kult: Divinity Lost

## The game
The world is an Illusion, a prison built to keep humanity asleep while the Demiurge is gone. Behind it lurk Archons, Death Angels and worse. The players are ordinary people whose dark secrets have begun to tear the Illusion open. The game is horror: cruel, personal and adult, and it should be played with safety tools the whole table agrees on.

## Rolling
A move is **2d10 + an attribute**. Each roll asks for a **modifier** (from relations, advantages, wounds, stress or the situation); enter the sum, or leave it at 0.
- **15+**: complete success.
- **10 to 14**: success with a cost, a hard choice or a complication.
- **9 or less**: failure; the GM makes a move, as hard as the fiction demands.

Some advantages and disadvantages roll 2d10 with no attribute.

## Attributes and basic moves
- Passive: **Fortitude** (Endure Injury), **Reflexes** (Avoid Harm), **Willpower** (Keep It Together).
- Active: **Reason** (Investigate), **Intuition** (Read a Person), **Perception** (Observe a Situation), **Coolness** (Act Under Pressure), **Violence** (Engage in Combat), **Charisma** (Influence Other), **Soul** (See Through the Illusion).

## Harm and wounds
Weapons deal **harm**. When a character is hurt, they roll **Endure Injury**: the roll asks for the harm and takes it off. A high result shrugs it off; lower results mean **serious wounds** (up to four) and then a **critical wound**, which kills unless it is **stabilized** soon. The battlemap's attack rows roll Engage in Combat and show the weapon's harm.

## Stability
Shocks, horror and violence call for **Keep It Together**. Failures drop **stability** from Composed through **moderate**, **serious** and **critical stress** to **Broken**. Stress makes ordinary actions harder, but opens the senses to what lies behind the Illusion. Conditions such as Angry or Guilt-ridden can be noted beside it.

## Dark secrets
Every character has dark secrets that the GM will bring into the story. They are the reason the Illusion is cracking around them.

There is no initiative: the conversation decides who acts.
