# Playing Cortex Prime

## Traits and dice
Everything a character is good at is a **trait** rated as a die: d4 (weak) through d6, d8, d10 to d12 (exceptional). Characters have **Distinctions** (three short phrases, each d8, or d4 to earn a plot point), **Attributes** and **Skills**. The GM can change the skill list in the campaign.

## Rolling
Build a **dice pool**: one die from each prime set (an attribute, a skill, a distinction if it fits), plus any **assets** and complications that apply. Pick the attribute, distinction and asset on the sheet, then roll a skill.

- Add the two best dice: that is the **total**.
- Keep one other die as the **effect die**; its size (not its number) matters. With no die left, the effect is d4. Fudger picks the best two for the total and the largest remaining die as effect.
- Each **1** is a **hitch**: the GM may give the player a plot point to make it a complication. All ones is a **botch**.
- Beat the opposition's total to succeed. Beating it by 5 or more is a **heroic success**: the effect die steps up.

The GM rolls an opposing pool, or sets a fixed difficulty (3 very easy, 7 easy, 11 challenging, 15 hard, 19 very hard).

## Plot points
Spend a plot point to add another die to the total, keep an extra effect die, or activate SFX. Earn them on hitches and by using a distinction as a d4.

## Stress and complications
The winner's effect die becomes **stress** (physical, mental or emotional) on the loser, or steps up stress already there. Stress stepped past d12 **takes the character out**. Complications are dice the GM adds to opposing pools until recovered.

## Turn order
No initiative: the GM picks who starts, and each character picks who acts next.
